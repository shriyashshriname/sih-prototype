# Maharashtra Disaster Research Data: Historical and Current Context

This document compiles historical and current disaster data for Maharashtra, specifically curated for ingestion into the SIH prototype backend dataset.

## 1. Major Flood and Landslide Events

### Malin Landslide (Pune, 2014)
* **Date:** July 30, 2014
* **Location:** Malin village, Ambegaon taluka, Pune district.
* **Casualties:** 151+ deaths.
* **Context/Triggers:** Heavy rainfall in the Western Ghats. Exacerbated by deforestation, changing agricultural practices (leveling steep slopes), and proximity to the Dimbhe Dam. Prompted state-wide geological stability surveys.

### Taliye Landslide (Raigad, 2021)
* **Date:** July 22, 2021
* **Location:** Taliye village, Mahad taluka, Raigad district.
* **Casualties:** 84–89 deaths.
* **Context/Triggers:** Buried under a massive debris flow during torrential Konkan monsoon rains. Highlighted the acute lack of early warning systems for hill-slope hamlets and urgent relocation needs.

### Chiplun Floods (Ratnagiri, 2021)
* **Date:** July 2021
* **Location:** Chiplun, Ratnagiri district.
* **Context/Triggers:** Unprecedented flooding (water levels rising over 20 feet in market areas), significantly surpassing the 2005 flood levels. Driven by intense rainfall, high tides, and dam water releases (Koyna Dam) inundating the Vashishti River basin.

### Kolhapur & Sangli Floods (2019 & 2021)
* **Dates:** Recurring major events in August 2019 and July 2021.
* **Context/Triggers:** Vulnerable due to heavy monsoons, overflow of Panchganga, Bhogawati, and Krishna rivers, and water discharge complexities from Almatti and Koyna dams. 2021 events resulted in over 250 deaths statewide and ₹4,000 crore in economic damage.

## 2. Government-Designated Relief Camps & Safe Sites

* **Activation & Setup:** There is no static public "live map" of relief camps. They are dynamically activated by District Collectors and the State Disaster Management Authority (SDMA). 
* **Typical Locations:** Government schools, community centers, and structurally sound public buildings situated on higher ground.
* **Capacities:** Capacities vary dynamically based on the scale of displacement and available district infrastructure. Large events are supplemented by NDRF/SDRF mobilization for tents, food, medical supplies, and sanitation.
* **Coordination:** Managed by the District Emergency Operation Center (1077) and State Emergency Operation Center (1070). Alerts are issued via geo-targeted SMS and official handles (@SDMAMaharashtra).

## 3. Real-World Evacuation Routes & Road Networks

### Vulnerable Mountain Passes (Ghats)
* **Tamhini Ghat:** Highly prone to landslides and cave-ins. Frequent closures are enforced by the Pune district administration during heavy monsoons (e.g., officially closed for significant periods in mid-to-late 2026 for structural safety).
* **Varandha Ghat (NH-965DD):** Faces routine closures throughout the monsoon season as a precautionary measure due to extremely high risks of landslides and falling debris. 

### Highways
* **NH-66 (Mumbai–Goa Highway):** A critical artery that suffers from waterlogging at low-lying river crossings. Subject to periodic traffic bans on heavy vehicles (16+ tonnes) during extreme weather or heavy festival traffic to avoid gridlocks.
* **NH-48 (Mumbai-Bengaluru Highway):** Crucial evacuation and supply route. While generally stable, specific structural repair works (e.g., Vaitarna Major Bridge) or heavy rains can cause localized bottlenecks.

## 4. River Gauges and Historical Danger Levels

Monitored by the Central Water Commission (CWC) and district disaster cells.

### Savitri River (Mahad Site, Raigad)
* **Danger Level:** 11.0 meters
* **Historical Context:** Reacts rapidly to Western Ghats rainfall. During the July 2021 floods, the river peaked at **12.75 meters**, bypassing the previous 2005 High Flood Level (HFL) of 11.93 meters.

### Panchganga River (Rajaram Weir Site, Kolhapur)
* **Warning Level:** 39 feet
* **Danger Level:** 43 feet
* **Historical Context:** Confluence of multiple streams (Kasari, Kumbhi, Tulsi, Bhogawati). Reached 44.7 feet in August 2020. Constantly breaches danger marks during heavy monsoons, forcing immediate village evacuations.

### Vashishti River (Chiplun, Ratnagiri)
* **Historical Context:** Swells extremely fast. Flooding disrupts the Chiplun market and Konkan Railway operations. Warning systems are heavily tied to local rainfall and upstream dam discharges.

### Krishna River (Irwin Bridge Site, Sangli)
* **Danger Level:** 45 feet
* **Historical Context:** Heavily influenced by upstream reservoirs and downstream dam discharges (Almatti Dam in Karnataka). Interstate coordination is vital to maintain water levels below the danger mark.
